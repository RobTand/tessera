# The fused routed window MoE lane (tessera#640), measured

Status: measurement record for the lane that contract v42 publishes. Every
number here was taken through PrismaBuild on the GLM serving image
`localhost/prismaquant/spark-vllm-nccl230@sha256:f8dbe1a02e33ccb7416ab40b72a83e8c725dcb6fed3e90bae4a658cce5e1b7f5`
(vLLM `0.28.1rc1.dev397+gfd4a15126.d20260904`, torch `2.13.0+cu130`) on a
GB10 (`sm_121`, 48 SMs, 140 W envelope). Every row named here has landed;
the receipts table at the end keys each section to its PrismaBuild action.

## What the lane is

`tessera.routed_fused.FusedRoutedWindowMoE` is a new launch identity for the
routed expert stacks of the two window families, `TESSERA_E4M3_K1` (the
epilogue arithmetic, extension `tessera_routed_fused_e4m3`) and
`TESSERA_BF16_K1` (the folded arithmetic, `tessera_routed_fused_value`). One
persistent, warp-specialised CUDA kernel (`routed_fused_kernel`, 512 threads,
BM 64 x BN 128 x BK 32, 97,616 B of shared memory) serves the gate and up
projections in one launch with the SwiGLU epilogue fused, and the down
projection in a second launch of the same kernel into route-sorted bf16 rows
that `token_sum_kernel` reduces in a fixed order. Work items are
`(expert, n-block, 64-route superblock)` triples claimed through a device
counter the caller zeroes in-stream, so a forward is CUDA-graph capturable and
never synchronises with the host. Every trellis window is decoded once per
tile and reused across up to 64 routes.

Two properties the compact adapter did not have, both tested on the image
(`tests/test_routed_fused_window.py`, 25 tests, PB row `9d7163d3...`):

- The down reduction is deterministic: two runs of the same forward are
  bitwise equal. The compact adapter's `index_add_` reduction is an fp32
  atomic add whose summation order depends on scheduling, so it is not.
- A captured graph replays twice with bitwise-equal output.

The lane is the default for every expert stack whose wire it reads (every
column at rate 4, window 14, identity order; the q256 1024 GLM stacks).
`TESSERA_ROUTED_FUSED=0` keeps the compact adapter; a stack the lane refuses
(the mixed-rate q256 896 rung, or a geometry outside the kernel's tile) stays
on the compact adapter with the reason logged; a box whose toolchain cannot
build the library falls back to the compact adapter at construction with a
warning, which is the `when_unavailable` substitution the extension entry
publishes.

### Reading

- Against the compact adapter, the lane this replaces on the same wire, the
  fused lane is 3.4x to 6.4x faster in wall time and 3.5x to 6.6x in kernel
  time for E4M3, 2.4x to 5.3x (wall) for BF16, at 71 W to 84 W against the
  compact adapter's 55 W to 73 W; forwards per joule improve 3.2x to 4.0x
  (E4M3) and 2.4x to 3.4x (BF16). This is the before/after the identity is
  judged on.
- Against vLLM's stock path over materialised weights, latency is matched or
  beaten for E4M3 at M <= 512 (5% to 20% faster) and 2.7% slower at M = 2048;
  for BF16 it is 1.9x to 2.5x faster at every M. **Forwards per joule are not
  matched**: the fused lane draws 0.51 to 0.60 of the envelope where the
  stock FP8 path draws 0.25 to 0.34, so at equal latency it spends about
  twice the energy (0.55x to 0.83x of stock's fwd/J for E4M3; 0.77x to 0.92x
  for BF16 below M = 2048, 1.19x at M = 2048). The stock kernel is
  memory-bound and leaves SMs idle; the trellis decode is compute the
  materialised path never pays. What the wire buys at these M is bytes (half
  of FP8, a quarter of BF16), not joules.
- The E4M3 lane at M = 1 runs at 51% of the DRAM roofline for the bytes it
  reads (the stock path runs at 93% of its own, reading twice the bytes);
  the decode, not the memory system, is the floor at small M. At M = 2048 it
  issues at 32% of the f16 MMA ceiling with the SwiGLU fused; the NCU rows
  below say where the rest goes.
- E2M1 is a gap, not a result: the native `a4_span2_grouped` path this lane
  does not cover is 2.5x (M = 1) to 7.3x (M = 2048) slower than the stock
  NVFP4 CUTLASS path and 3x to 6x worse per joule, and its `index_add_`
  reduction is order-dependent. A fused E2M1 identity is the next kernel.
- All legs are eager. Wall time includes each leg's launch gaps; the kernel
  column is the graph-mode proxy.
- 16 of 288 experts are loaded, so per-expert row counts at large M are
  denser than a 288-expert serve. The PACT bench below covers the served
  per-rank shapes.

What the counters say:

- The fused kernel runs one 16-warp block per SM: NCU's block limits are 1 by
  registers (98 x 512 threads of the 64K file), 1 by shared memory (97.4 KB
  dynamic) and 3 by warps, so theoretical occupancy is 33.3% and achieved
  equals it. Its busiest unit is L1/TEX at 50% to 60% of peak, with the SM
  (tensor) pipes at 28% to 33% and L2 at 20% to 31%: the kernel is bound on
  the shared-memory and L1 data path that stages decoded weights and the
  route-gathered A tiles, not on MMA issue or DRAM. L2 hit rates of 59% to
  77% say the re-reads of an expert's wire across its 64-route superblocks
  are mostly served from L2. NCU's TheoreticalOccupancy rule estimates 66.7%
  local headroom; the next steps are a register budget that admits a second
  block (at most 64 per thread) or a smaller shared-memory footprint, and a
  wider MMA tile so each staged byte feeds more issue.
- `token_sum_kernel` is a tail of 0.6% to 4% of the forward's kernel time
  (0.14 ms at M = 512, 0.63 ms at M = 2048), memory-side at 12% to 14% of
  peak; it is the price of the deterministic reduction.
- The compact adapter's Triton kernel holds 169 to 194 registers, so one
  8-warp block per SM (16.7% occupancy), and its grid spans all 288 experts
  times every route block (12,288 to 98,304 waves per SM at these M) of
  which most exit empty on this 16-expert load: the launch-shape cost the
  persistent work counter removes. It reads through L1 at 92% to 96% hit and
  L2 at 1% to 18%, with the SM pipes at 13% to 24%; it re-decodes the window
  for every route block it visits.
