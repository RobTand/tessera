# Tessera-16 run tables: the geometry sweep and the allowable rule (2026-09-30, tessera#750)

**Status:** draft. The attribution of the two-run cost (NCU at M=512) and the
rate-8 down rerun are pending; the sections that depend on them say so.

This receipt backs `formats[TESSERA_BF16_K1].allowable_rungs` for the folded
T-16 lanes (the value family: BF16 tables with the row scale folded in,
`bf16` `mma.sync`). It follows the #750 sweep protocol so that the T-4, T-8
and T-16 sets can be reconciled.

## Summary

- **Every one-run table `[1]`..`[8]` is fast.** On the routed lane, rates 1-6
  run within 22% of each other at every M. Rates 7 and 8 run 19-28% slower
  than rate 6 on gate/up at M ≤ 512 (5% at M=8192), because their 16-word slot
  fits only two word stages there (WP7, contract v48).
- **A two-run table `[r, r+1]` costs one number per (table, M), whatever its
  mix.** Inside a table, the three swept mixes (hi = 0.25, 0.50, 0.75) take the
  same time within the spread. So the time model is per table, and an
  exclusion is naturally a table exclusion.
- **Two-run tables at three word stages cost 21-39% more than the one-run
  table above them (`[r+1]`), at every M, on the routed lane.** Where the
  one-run neighbours already run two word stages (`[6,7]`, `[7,8]` gate/up)
  the cost is 6-9% at M ≤ 512 and 24-33% at M=8192. On the dense lane it is
  23-36% at M ≤ 512 and a tie at M=8192 (`q_b`: 6-14%).
- **Under a table-level reading of the exclusion rule, the routed two-run
  tables `[1,2]`..`[6,7]` are excluded on today's kernel; `[7,8]` waits on the
  rate-8 down rerun.** Under a literal rung-by-rung reading on the 64-step
  grid, only the hi = 0.75 rung of each table is excluded. See
  [Exclusion](#exclusion).
- **Rates above 8:** rates 15 and 16 are excluded by table geometry. Rates
  12-14 are dominated by BF16 passthrough on extrapolated time. Rates 9-11 are
  a real decode window (extrapolated 0.76-0.97x of BF16 at M ≤ 512), but no
  layout of today's gate/up launch fits them. They are not excluded; they are
  not built. See [Rates above 8](#rates-above-8).

### Linearity inside a table

Time is not linear in the mix between a table's one-run neighbours: it is flat
across the mix and above the interpolation. Within each table, the hi = 0.25,
0.50 and 0.75 rungs agree within the spread at every M, on both launches. For
example gate/up at M=64: q320 13.52, q384 13.37, q448 13.39 ms; q1088 13.77,

## Exclusion

The #750 rule: exclude a rung when the next supported rung above it, within
one grid step, is at least as fast at every M, beyond the spread.

**Reading 1, rung by rung on the 64-step grid.** Excluded: the hi = 0.75 rung
of every table (q448, 704, 960, 1216, 1472, 1728 on the routed total; q1984
too on gate/up). Their upper neighbour is the one-run table `[r+1]`. Every
other two-run rung ties its upper neighbour (the next mix in the same table)
and stays. The result depends on the grid step: at `step_q256 = 1` only q256 =
256r + 255 would be excluded.

**Reading 2, by table (`excluded_run_tables`).** A table's time does not
depend on its mix, so a table is excluded when the one-run table above it is
at least as fast at every M, beyond the spread. This reading does not depend
on the grid step, and it matches the lead's mapping of a slow table onto
`excluded_run_tables`. On today's kernel:

- Routed: `[1,2]`..`[6,7]` are excluded (the upper table is 7-38% faster at
  every M, against spreads of 0.1-4.7%). `[7,8]` ties `[8]` at M ≤ 512 and
  loses by 32% at M=8192; the tie rests on the rate-8 down cell, so its
  verdict waits on the rerun.
- Dense: the upper table is 23-36% faster at M ≤ 512 and ties at M=8192 on
  `o_proj` and KDA. "At least as fast" holds; "faster beyond the spread at
  every M" does not. The rule's wording has to pick one. If the dense lane
  keeps the two-run tables, it keeps them for prefill, where the package-3
  gap (below) makes the whole dense lane 1.8-4.8x slower than BF16 anyway.

**What exclusion costs the allocator.**

- The byte budget keeps its resolution either way. Units choose rates
  independently, so a budget between two integer rates is met by putting
  some units on each. One routed projection of one layer is about 2.4 B
  parameters at TP1; one bit per weight on it is 0.3 GB.
- What the allocator loses is the accuracy of a fractional rung against the
  chord of its whole-rate neighbours. If a unit's error at q = 256r + x lies on
  or above the straight line between rates r and r+1, integer rungs mixed
  across units buy the same accuracy per byte, and the two-run tables buy
  nothing for their 33-44%. `experiments/rung_chord.py` (T-8) measures that
  chord on E4M3. *Pending: the same measurement on the value family.*
- Excluding all seven tables on the routed lane leaves a per-unit grid step of
  256 (1 bpw). The largest gap is 1 bpw everywhere.

**Proposed reading:** reading 2, routed `[1,2]`..`[6,7]` excluded, `[7,8]`
after the rerun, conditional on the NCU attribution. If the two-run cost is
this implementation rather than the table's geometry, the tables stay
allowable and the time table prices them at their measured cost; the ruling
excludes only rungs whose geometry makes them intrinsically worse.

## Rates above 8

The BF16_K1 wire reads q256 256-4096, but no Tessera kernel decodes a rate
above 8 (`RATE_MAX` in `routed_fused_window.cu`,
`compact_prep.WINDOW_GEMM_RATE_MAX`).

| rate | window bits L | fused table per projection | gate/up smem at 2 stages | down smem at 3 stages | routed time / BF16, extrapolated (M=1, 64, 512) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 9, 10 | 14 | 32 KB | 101,840 (+464 over) | 74,000 | 0.81-0.89, 0.81-0.89, 0.76-0.84 |
| 11, 12 | 14 | 32 KB | 103,888 (+2,512 over) | 77,072 | 0.97-1.04, 0.97-1.05, 0.92-1.00 |
| 13, 14 | 14 | 32 KB | 105,936 (+4,560 over) | 80,144 | 1.12-1.20, 1.12-1.20, 1.07-1.15 |
| 15, 16 | 15, 16 | 64, 128 KB | does not fit | does not fit | - |

- **15 and 16: excluded by geometry.** The exporter widens the window to the
  rate above 14 (`export._window_bits_for`), so the table is 2^15 or 2^16
  entries. Two of them cannot fit a block, and the fused lane requires
  `window_bits` 14.
- **12-14: dominated by BF16 passthrough.** The extrapolation (the routed
  total's slope over rates 5-8, per M) puts them at or above BF16's time at
  every M, and at M=8192 rate 8 already ties BF16. BF16 passthrough is exact,
  so these rungs are slower and less accurate.
- **9-11: not excluded, not built.** They are 0.76-0.97x of BF16 at decode by
  extrapolation, with 56-69% of its bytes, and accuracy above rate 8 is
  measurable (T-16 at rate 7 served KL 0.0049 on Qwen3-0.6B against 0.0205
  for FP8 RTN W8A8;
  `docs/measurements/tessera-bf16-route-served-2026-09-02.md`). But the
  two-table gate/up launch does not fit a 20-word slot at two word stages
  (464 B over at rates 9-10), so they need a layout change there (for example
  one table per launch) and `RATE_MAX` raised through the window stack. The
  down launch fits them at three stages today.
- The extrapolation assumes the rate-5-to-8 slope holds; it is an estimate,
  not a measurement. The rate-8 row includes the rate-8 down cell above.
