# Tessera-16 run tables: the geometry sweep and the allowable rule (2026-09-30, tessera#750)

**Status:** draft. Three measurements are pending: the attribution of the
two-run cost (NCU at M=512), the rate-8 down rerun, and the chord test on the
BF16 grid. The sections that depend on them say so. Rates 9-14 on the
one-table launch are not measured yet; the kernel does not decode them.

This receipt backs `formats[TESSERA_BF16_K1].allowable_rungs` for the folded
T-16 lanes (the value family: BF16 tables with the row scale folded in,
`bf16` `mma.sync`). It follows the #750 sweep protocol so that the T-4, T-8
and T-16 sets can be reconciled.

## Summary

- **Every one-run table `[1]`..`[8]` is fast.** On the routed lane, rates 1-6
  run within 22% of each other at every M. Rates 7 and 8 run 19-28% slower
  than rate 6 on gate/up at M ≤ 512 (5% at M=8192), because their 16-word slot
  fits only two word stages there (WP7, contract v48).
- **A two-run table `[r, r+1]` costs one number per (table, M), whatever its
  mix.** Inside a table, the three swept mixes (hi = 0.25, 0.50, 0.75) take the
  same time within the spread. So the time model is per table, and an
  exclusion is naturally a table exclusion.
- **Two-run tables at three word stages cost 21-39% more than the one-run
  table above them (`[r+1]`), at every M, on the routed lane.** Where the
  one-run neighbours already run two word stages (`[6,7]`, `[7,8]` gate/up)
  the cost is 6-9% at M ≤ 512 and 24-33% at M=8192. On the dense lane it is
  23-36% at M ≤ 512 and a tie at M=8192 (`q_b`: 6-14%).
- **No table in `[1]`..`[8]` is excluded.** Rob's ruling (2026-09-30) makes
  every valid rate allowable unless its geometry makes it intrinsically worse.
  The two-run cost is priced by the time table rather than excluded, as on T-8.
  A two-run table buys granularity that a whole-rate mix does not. See
  [Exclusion](#exclusion).
- **Rates above 8 split by launch.** See [Rates above 8](#rates-above-8).
  - **16: excluded.** It reads BF16's bytes, and BF16 passthrough is exact.
  - **15: excluded by geometry.** Its window is 15 bits, so each table is
    64 KB. The one-table launch fits it only with one word stage, and the
    two-table launch does not fit it at all.
  - **9-14 on the one-table launch (dense, routed down): fits at three word
    stages** (at most 80,144 B of 101,376 B). No kernel decodes these rates
    yet, so they are not measured. The extension is being built for the
    dense lane to measure them.
  - **9-14 on the two-table launch (routed gate/up): does not fit** at two
    word stages (464-4,560 B over). That is this launch's layout, not the
    rate's geometry: one table per launch would fit. Until such a launch
    exists, the route is unbacked.

### Linearity inside a table

Time is not linear in the mix between a table's one-run neighbours: it is flat
across the mix and above the interpolation. Within each table, the hi = 0.25,
0.50 and 0.75 rungs agree within the spread at every M, on both launches. For
example gate/up at M=64: q320 13.52, q384 13.37, q448 13.39 ms; q1088 13.77,
q1152 13.62, q1216 13.74 ms.

- One-run dense time is flat across rates 1-8 at every M (`o_proj` M=1:
  0.091-0.104 ms).
- Two-run dense tables cost 23-36% over the one-run table above them at

- The `q_b` BF16 reference at M=1 reads its 25 MB weight at about 466 GB/s,

## Exclusion

The #750 sweep rule excludes a rung when the next supported rung above it is
at least as fast at every M. Rob's ruling (2026-09-30) narrows it: every valid
rate is allowable, except rates whose geometry makes them intrinsically worse.

**Reading adopted: no table in `[1]`..`[8]` is excluded.** This matches T-8
(`2026-09-30-t8-run-tables.md`). Every q256 in [256, 2048] at step 1 is
allowable on the routed and dense lanes, and the time table prices each table
at its measured cost.

- **The two-run cost is not shown to be intrinsic.**
  - A two-run chunk maps its columns at run time and decodes two rates, so it
    does cost something.
  - But the measured cost ranges from -5% to +38% by table and launch.
  - It falls to 6-9% on gate/up where the one-run neighbours already run two
    word stages (`[6,7]`, `[7,8]`).
  - That pattern points at pipeline depth and per-chunk synchronization in
    this implementation. The NCU attribution (PB `4c3d7680`, pending) tests
    it.
- **A two-run table buys granularity.**
  - A grouped routed stack holds one packed layout per projection across its
    288 experts, so each projection is one unit of about 2.4 G weights.
  - A whole-bit step on that unit costs about 302 MB (T-8's chord section).
  - The allocator can weigh that granularity against the table's time only if
    the table is allowable.
- **Whether a fractional rung buys accuracy per byte** is the chord test. On
  E4M3, T-8 measured a fractional rung on the chord of its whole rates (within
  1% of the pair's error drop), so it buys granularity and no accuracy. The
  same test on the BF16 grid is pending (PB `ee8bd15c`).
- **The strict wording would keep the dense tables anyway.** "The rung above
  is faster beyond the spread at every M" fails on the dense lane, where the
  two-run tables tie `[r+1]` at M=8192.

Two earlier readings, from before the ruling, are kept for the record. Read
rung by rung on the 64-step grid, the rule excluded the hi = 0.75 rung of each
table. Read table by table, it excluded the routed tables `[1,2]`..`[6,7]`.

## Rates above 8

The BF16_K1 wire reads q256 256-4096, and the exporter encodes every rate up
to 16. It widens the window to the rate above 14 (`export._window_bits_for`).
No Tessera kernel decodes a rate above 8: `RATE_MAX` in
`routed_fused_window.cu` and `compact_prep.WINDOW_GEMM_RATE_MAX` are both 8.

Shared memory per block, from the layout constants in `routed_fused_window.cu`
(`OFF_W` 58,640 B with one 32 KB table, 91,600 B with two; 768 B per slot word
at three word stages, 512 B at two). A pair's slot is the larger of its two
rates' slots, so a pair falls in the row of its upper rate.

| rate | window bits | table | one-table launch (dense, routed down) | two-table launch (routed gate/up) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 9, 10 | 14 | 32 KB | 3 stages: 74,000 B | 2 stages: 101,840 B (464 over) |
| 11, 12 | 14 | 32 KB | 3 stages: 77,072 B | 2 stages: 103,888 B (2,512 over) |
| 13, 14 | 14 | 32 KB | 3 stages: 80,144 B | 2 stages: 105,936 B (4,560 over) |
| 15 | 15 | 64 KB | 1 stage: 99,600 B; 2 stages: 107,792 B (6,416 over) | does not fit (two 64 KB tables) |
| 16 | 16 | 128 KB | does not fit | does not fit |

The block limit is 101,376 B (sm_121 opt-in). At 128-row superblocks
(BM_WIDE) the one-table launch at rate 14 takes 96,528 B, which also fits.

- **16: excluded.** A rate-16 unit reads exactly BF16's bytes, and BF16
  passthrough decodes them exactly. Its 128 KB table also fits no block.
- **15: excluded by geometry.** Its 64 KB table leaves room for one word stage
  on the one-table launch, so the decode cannot overlap the word stream. The
  two-table launch cannot hold it. It would save 6% of BF16's bytes, with
  nonzero error, against an exact passthrough. The pair `[14,15]` has the same
  15-bit window, so it is excluded with rate 15.
- **9-14, one-table launch: fits; not measured.** The one-run dense time is
  flat across rates 1-8 at M ≤ 64 (`o_proj` at M=1: 0.091-0.104 ms, against
  0.207 ms for BF16). That makes rates 9-14 a strong candidate at decode, but
  flat through rate 8 is not a measurement at rate 14. The extension to
  `RATE_MAX` 14 on the value family's one-table launch is the measurement.
  Its tables would be `[1]`..`[14]` and the pairs up to `[13,14]`.
- **9-14, two-table launch: unbacked.** The fused gate/up layout does not fit
  a 20-28-word slot at two word stages, and one stage would serialize the
  decode. Splitting gate and up into two one-table launches would fit at three
  stages, so the limit belongs to this launch and not to the rate. Until a
  launch serves these rates, a routed gate/up unit above rate 8 on T-16 has no
  backed route. Under the producer's route-status gate that fails export
  closed; it is not an exclusion.

How the contract expresses the split (contract v48 on T-8's branch):

- The family's `allowable_rungs` rule bounds what a reader may take. For
  T-16 it would be q256 256-3584 with the tables `[1]`..`[14]` and the pairs
  up to `[13,14]`.
- A `lane_eligibility` cell covers only the run tables of its own census
  rungs (`run_tables`, derived by the validator). So dense cells can reach
  `[14]` while routed cells stop at `[8]`, with no new field.
- A `routed_moe` cell covers the gate/up and down launches together, so it
  cannot admit down above rate 8 without gate/up. This package builds rates
  9-14 for the dense lane only, so the gap does not arise. Routed down above
  8 would need a per-launch field first.
